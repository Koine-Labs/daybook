Frontal-midline theta power increases with working-memory and sustained-attention
demand, while beta-band changes yield a rising frontal theta/beta ratio under
increasing cognitive load. The measure is electrode-placement sensitive and easily
contaminated by ocular and muscle artifacts. Critically, the theta/beta ratio also
rises with drowsiness, so an elevated ratio must be disambiguated from low-arousal
states before being read as cognitive load.
