Source: Juslin PN, Scherer KR. Vocal expression of affect. In: The New Handbook of
Methods in Nonverbal Behavior Research, 2005. (curated excerpt — local)

High-arousal affective states are reliably marked by increased speech rate and elevated
high-frequency spectral energy in the voice. These acoustic cues generalize across
cultures but show heavy language, dialect, and individual-baseline variation, and are
sensitive to microphone characteristics and speaker distance. Speech rate also rises
with task urgency independent of affect, so the cue is weak on its own.
