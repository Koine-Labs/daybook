Across the pooled studies, acute psychological stress was associated with decreased
high-frequency (HF) HRV power, indicating reduced parasympathetic activity and a shift
toward sympathetic dominance. Tonic heart-rate elevation co-occurred with sympathetic
arousal when metabolic demand (physical exertion) was controlled for. Effect sizes were
heterogeneous across stress paradigms; HF-HRV estimates are strongly modulated by
respiration rate and posture, and short measurement windows are noisy.
